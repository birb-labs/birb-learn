:::question kp-01
According to Kepler's 1st law, the orbits of the planets around the Sun are:
---options---
- circles, with the Sun at the center.
- ellipses, with the Sun at one of the foci.
- ellipses, with the Sun at the center.
- parabolas, with the Sun at the vertex.
---resolution---
**Kepler's 1st law (law of orbits):** each planet moves in an **elliptical** orbit around the Sun, with the Sun at **one of the foci** (not at the center). That is why the distance between the planet and the Sun changes along the orbit.

**Answer:** ellipses, with the Sun at one of the foci.
:::

:::question kp-02
At which point of its orbit does a planet move **fastest**?
---options---
- At perihelion (the point closest to the Sun).
- At aphelion (the point farthest from the Sun).
- The speed is the same at every point.
- At the points halfway between perihelion and aphelion.
---resolution---
By **Kepler's 2nd law**, the Sun–planet line sweeps out equal areas in equal times. Near the Sun, this line is shorter, so the planet has to cover a **longer** arc in the same time to sweep the same area: it is **fastest at perihelion** and slowest at aphelion.

**Answer:** At perihelion (the point closest to the Sun).
:::

:::question kp-03
The seasons are mainly caused by the changing distance between the Earth and the Sun along the orbit.
---resolution---
**False.** Earth's orbit is almost circular (eccentricity of about 0.017), and the Earth is **closest** to the Sun in early January, when it is summer in the Southern Hemisphere but winter in the Northern Hemisphere. If distance were the cause, both hemispheres would have the same season at the same time. The seasons are caused by the **tilt of the Earth's rotation axis**.
:::

:::question kp-04
An asteroid orbits the Sun with a semi-major axis of $9\ \text{AU}$. Using Kepler's 3rd law in the form $T^2 = a^3$ (with $T$ in Earth years and $a$ in astronomical units), what is the asteroid's period, in years?
---resolution---
$$T^2 = a^3 = 9^3 = 729 \quad\Rightarrow\quad T = \sqrt{729} = 27\ \text{years}$$

**Another way:** $T = a^{3/2} = (\sqrt{9})^3 = 3^3 = 27$.

**Answer:** $27$ years.
:::

:::question kp-05
A comet takes $8$ years to complete one orbit around the Sun. Using $T^2 = a^3$ ($T$ in years and $a$ in AU), what is the semi-major axis of its orbit, in AU?
---resolution---
$$a^3 = T^2 = 8^2 = 64 \quad\Rightarrow\quad a = \sqrt[3]{64} = 4\ \text{AU}$$

**Answer:** $4\ \text{AU}$.
:::

:::question kp-06
Satellite A moves in a circular orbit of radius $R$ around the Earth, with period $T$. Satellite B moves in a circular orbit of radius $4R$ around the Earth. B's period is:
---options---
- $2T$
- $4T$
- $8T$
- $16T$
---resolution---
Both orbit the **same** Earth, so the ratio $\dfrac{T^2}{R^3}$ is the same for both:

$$\frac{T_B^2}{(4R)^3} = \frac{T^2}{R^3} \quad\Rightarrow\quad T_B^2 = 64\,T^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad T_B = 8T$$

**Answer:** $8T$.
:::

:::question kp-07
A planet is $2\ \text{AU}$ from the Sun at perihelion and $6\ \text{AU}$ at aphelion. How many times greater is its speed at perihelion than at aphelion?
---resolution---
By Kepler's 2nd law, at perihelion and aphelion (where the velocity is perpendicular to the Sun–planet line):

$$r_p\,v_p = r_a\,v_a \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{v_p}{v_a} = \frac{r_a}{r_p} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$$

**Answer:** $3$ times.
:::

:::question kp-08
Kepler's 2nd law states that:
---options---
- the square of the period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis.
- the line segment joining the Sun and the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal intervals of time.
- the planet's speed is constant along the orbit.
- all planets have the same period.
---resolution---
**2nd law (law of areas):** the Sun–planet line sweeps out **equal areas in equal times**. One consequence is that the speed is **not** constant: the planet is faster at perihelion.

The first option is the **3rd law**.

**Answer:** the line segment joining the Sun and the planet sweeps out equal areas in equal intervals of time.
:::

:::question kp-09
A body orbits the Sun with perihelion at $2\ \text{AU}$ and aphelion at $8\ \text{AU}$. What is the eccentricity of the orbit? (Answer with a decimal number.)
---resolution---
**1. Semi-major axis:**

$$a = \frac{r_p + r_a}{2} = \frac{2 + 8}{2} = 5\ \text{AU}$$

**2. Focal distance** (from the center of the ellipse to the focus, where the Sun is):

$$c = a - r_p = 5 - 2 = 3\ \text{AU}$$

**3. Eccentricity:**

$$e = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6$$

**Answer:** $0.6$.
:::

:::question kp-10
The constant $K = T^2/a^3$ of Kepler's 3rd law has the same value for the Moon (orbiting the Earth) and for Mars (orbiting the Sun).
---resolution---
**False.** The 3rd-law constant is the same only for bodies orbiting the **same central body**. From Newton's gravitation, $K = \dfrac{4\pi^2}{G M}$, where $M$ is the mass of the central body. The Moon orbits the Earth and Mars orbits the Sun, which is much more massive, so the constants are different.
:::

:::question kp-11
A planet takes $30$ days to sweep an area $A$ (with the line joining it to the Sun) near perihelion. How long does it take to sweep an area $2A$ near aphelion?
---options---
- $15$ days
- $30$ days
- $60$ days
- $120$ days
---resolution---
By the 2nd law, the area swept is **proportional to the time** in any part of the orbit (the areal velocity is constant):

$$\frac{A}{30} = \frac{2A}{t} \quad\Rightarrow\quad t = 60\ \text{days}$$

The position in the orbit does not change this proportion: near aphelion the planet is slower, but the Sun–planet line is longer, and the two effects balance out.

**Answer:** $60$ days.
:::

:::question kp-12
Select **all** the correct statements.
---options---
- The orbits of the planets are elliptical.
- The square of a planet's period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit.
- A planet's speed is constant along its orbit.
- The Sun is at the center of the ellipse traced by each planet.
- Kepler's laws also apply to satellites orbiting the Earth.
---resolution---
- **Elliptical orbits:** correct (1st law).
- **$T^2 \propto a^3$:** correct (3rd law).
- **Constant speed:** incorrect. By the 2nd law, the planet is faster at perihelion.
- **Sun at the center:** incorrect. The Sun is at one of the **foci**.
- **Earth satellites:** correct. The laws hold for any body orbiting a central body; only the 3rd-law constant changes.
:::

Match each item to its description.

---resolution---

:::question kp-14
Comparing two planets in the Solar System, the one **farther** from the Sun has:
---options---
- a shorter period and a higher average orbital speed.
- a longer period and a lower average orbital speed.
- a longer period and a higher average orbital speed.
- the same period, since Kepler's constant is the same.
---resolution---
- **Period:** by the 3rd law, $T^2 \propto a^3$; the larger $a$, the larger $T$.
- **Speed:** in a roughly circular orbit, $v = \dfrac{2\pi a}{T}$. Since $T \propto a^{3/2}$, we get $v \propto \dfrac{a}{a^{3/2}} = a^{-1/2}$: the farther away, the **slower**. (From Newton's gravitation, $v = \sqrt{GM/r}$, the same result.)

Example: Mercury completes one orbit in about 88 days, and Neptune in about 165 years.

**Answer:** a longer period and a lower average orbital speed.
:::
